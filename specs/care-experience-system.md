# Letter Care Experience System

Status: approved product direction; usability validation pending
Date: 2026-07-28

## Product Objective

Care borrows the immediacy of short-video products and the feedback clarity of
games without borrowing their engagement objective.

Short-video objective:

> Keep the person consuming the next item.

Letter objective:

> Make the person feel met within seconds, move them toward one useful action,
> and make it easy to leave the screen within 20 to 90 seconds.

Session length is not a success metric. A short Care session followed by a
real-world comfort action can be the best outcome.

## The Finite Reward Loop

Every Care experience uses the same six-beat grammar:

```text
NAME IT
  one tap selects the state
    |
    v
RESPOND
  visual or tactile feedback begins immediately
    |
    v
TRANSFORM
  the world visibly moves from the current state toward the needed state
    |
    v
PROTECT
  one short line validates or creates a boundary
    |
    v
HAND OFF
  return one personal action, message, or note
    |
    v
EXIT
  start the real-world action or close the app
```

The product may offer a later `better`, `same`, or `worse` check-back. It must
not ask for reflection during the acute loop.

## Reward Layers

### 1. Sensory Reward

The first tap produces an immediate, legible response through animation,
optional sound, or optional haptics.

### 2. Agency Reward

The interface obeys the user's gesture. Chaos organizes, pressure releases,
noise stops, a boundary closes, or a rough object becomes smooth.

### 3. Emotional Reward

Letter gives one protective line. It does not teach or argue.

### 4. Personal Reward

After the first cycle, the strongest reward is not generic content. It is a
specific note or action from the user's own history.

### 5. Real-World Reward

The final control starts something outside the screen: fetch heat, lie down,
send a prepared boundary, dim the room, drink water, or postpone a decision.

## Anti-Addiction Rules

- no infinite feed
- no streak, coins, leaderboard, or failure state
- no reward for reporting greater suffering
- no random provocative content
- no autoplay from one emotional mode into another
- no more than three protective copy cards in one acute session
- no hidden exit or forced completion
- no notification designed to pull a stable user back into distress content

Retention should come from remembered personal value across cycles, not
variable entertainment rewards.

## Care Gate

User-facing choices describe experience instead of diagnosis:

1. `I want to explode`
2. `I feel heavy`
3. `My mind won't stop`
4. `I need everyone away`
5. `My body hurts`

Each choice has a visible text label and a supporting icon. The user's two most
frequent choices may appear first after enough history, but the product never
infers and opens a mode automatically.

## Mode 1: Shatter And Seal

State: rage, volatile energy, or a strong impulse to send, post, quit, buy, or
end something.

Desired transformation:

```text
HIGH ENERGY -> SUDDEN SILENCE -> TIME BETWEEN FEELING AND ACTION
```

### Immediate Scene

- one abstract crystal fills most of the screen
- taps create local cracks with optional glass sound and haptics
- the object responds on every tap; controls do not shake
- the sequence ends at 20 seconds or when the object breaks
- skip and urgent-safety controls remain available

### Transition

The object collapses and all sensory feedback stops. The screen cuts to a dim,
quiet surface.

Suggested copy:

> Done. The glass took the hit. Your life doesn't have to.

> Don't send it. Don't post it. Don't quit tonight.

> Write it here exactly as it came. No recipient. Nothing leaves Letter.

### Hand-Off

The user writes or dictates an unsent draft, reviews the exact consequence, and
chooses `Seal for 24 hours`.

While sealed, Letter prevents read, copy, share, and export inside the app. The
unopened envelope can always be deleted. After 24 hours the user chooses open,
keep sealed, delete, rewrite, or convert to a future-self note.

The Shatter scene is an expressive interaction, not a claim that aggressive
catharsis reduces anger.

## Mode 2: A Light For Me

State: crying, emptiness, low energy, hopelessness, or feeling unable to move.

Desired transformation:

```text
NOTHING IN ME -> THE SMALLEST INPUT COUNTS -> I AM NOT ALONE
```

### Immediate Scene

A dark, still pool contains one dim light in the bottom thumb zone.

- one tap wakes the light
- the light continues pulsing without requiring a continuous hold
- optional haptics use a slow, steady cadence
- a slow swipe may expand the light, but no gesture is mandatory
- the scene never fails or resets

The central game reward is that almost no effort produces a visible response.

### Protective Cards

Show one line at a time, with a maximum of three:

> You do not have to become okay all at once.

> Nothing needs to be solved from this minute.

> Stay for one small wave. Then we can choose what is next.

When available, replace generic copy with a user-approved note from a clearer
day:

> Last time you asked me to remind you: "This feeling got lighter by morning."

Do not claim that biology is the only cause or that the state will definitely
end at a specific time.

### Hand-Off

Offer three large choices:

- `Stay with me for 2 minutes`
- `Bring me my Care Kit`
- `Nothing else right now`

`I may not be safe` remains visible and replaces the normal flow with
deterministic locale-aware support.

## Mode 3: Make It Smaller

State: racing thoughts, anxiety, cognitive overload, or many simultaneous
demands.

Desired transformation:

```text
EVERYTHING AT ONCE -> ONE POINT -> ONE NEXT THING OR NOTHING NOW
```

### Immediate Scene

Loose lines and fragments move around one calm center point.

- pressing anywhere causes fragments to snap toward the center
- continued contact slows the scene, but a single tap can complete it
- visual complexity decreases with every interaction
- haptic cadence slows rather than intensifies
- no breathing instruction is mandatory

Suggested copy:

> Your mind opened every tab at once. We only need one.

> The rest can wait outside this minute.

### Current-Session Set-Down

The user may type one thought or tap `I don't want to name it`. In P0 this text
exists only on the current screen and is cleared when set down, discarded, or
left. Letter does not save or bring it back tomorrow.

Choices:

- `Set it down for now`
- `I don't want to name it`
- `Nothing now`

This is not a task manager or future reminder. Its job is to make naming
optional while keeping the interaction finite.

### Hand-Off

P0 returns to the Care choices or closes Letter with no further action. Saved
grounding environments and cross-session memory remain separate features.

## Mode 4: Close The Curtain

State: social overload, resentment from being available, or needing space
without creating unnecessary conflict.

Desired transformation:

```text
EVERYONE HAS ACCESS -> I CLOSE A BOUNDARY -> THE BOUNDARY HAS AN END
```

### Immediate Scene

A short downward pull or one tap closes a heavy curtain across the screen.

- the curtain follows the user's finger
- background silhouettes and sound disappear
- reduced-motion users receive a simple crossfade

Suggested copy:

> The door is closed. You are allowed to be unavailable.

The experience is symbolic and must state:

> Letter can quiet this screen. It cannot silence calls or other apps.

### Prepared Boundary

After the cocoon closes, the user may choose `Prepare words` or
`Nothing else right now`. The words path shows at most two static, truthful,
editable templates and three bounded durations: `30 minutes`, `2 hours`, and
`4 hours`. It has no recipient field. Letter does not invent a migraine,
illness, or claim of safety.

Example:

> I need some quiet time for the next 2 hours. I will not be available to
> reply.

An explicit `Copy text` action may write only the visible text to the operating
system clipboard after disclosing that it may remain there after Letter closes.
Letter does not read contacts or the existing clipboard, send or share,
open a composer or another app, or claim delivery. Letter-side text is cleared
when the Care session ends.

### Hand-Off

The user may finish directly from the cocoon without preparing or copying
words. Every path ends on one quiet screen with explicit return-to-Care and
leave-Care actions. P0 does not control Focus settings, keep a prepared message,
schedule a reminder, or record whether the boundary helped.

## Mode 5: Physical Care

State: cramps, back pain, headache or migraine, nausea, breast tenderness,
muscle or joint pain, or severe physical depletion.

The first Care response is comfort, not a form. A visible control always allows:

> This is new, unusual, or severe.

Selecting it replaces the normal scene with deterministic medical routing.

### Cramps Or Lower-Back Pain: Melt The Knot

Desired transformation:

```text
TIGHT AND SHARP -> WARM AND SMOOTH -> FETCH WHAT HELPED BEFORE
```

- one rough knot or stone responds to a slow trace
- it warms and softens visually
- one tap can enable automatic continuation when movement is difficult
- haptics and sound are optional and make no analgesic claim

The reward is a precise personal action:

> Last time, heat helped a little. Your note says: "Plug-in pad in the couch
> basket."

Primary control:

`I'm getting it now`

Then Letter offers a user-chosen comfort interval and a later effect check.

### Headache Or Migraine: Blackout

The correct game-design decision is less interaction.

- immediately dim the app surface
- disable sound, animation, and haptics
- show one saved action in very large text
- offer `Close my eyes for 10 minutes`
- avoid swiping, tracing, flashing, and decorative motion

A sudden explosive or worst-ever headache, or a headache with neurological
changes, confusion, fever/stiff neck, head injury, or similar warning signs
must route to urgent medical guidance.

### Nausea Or Bloating: Settle The Water

- a disturbed horizontal surface settles after one tap
- do not use tilt controls or camera motion
- show one saved position, drink, food, heat, or environment action
- provide a quiet timed interval

### Breast, Muscle, Or Joint Discomfort: Unknot

- one slow horizontal gesture loosens a tight thread
- automatic completion remains available
- return a saved clothing, support, warmth/coolness, massage, rest, or gentle
  movement preference

### Physical Depletion: Power Down

- one large switch closes visual "open tabs"
- no performance goal or sleep claim
- start the user's saved rest setup
- offer a truthful work or social boundary message

### Medication Scope

Letter does not record medication history or provide medication names, doses,
intervals, reminders, interaction advice, or medication timers.

## Later Reflection

Reflection begins only after the user chooses to reopen the event or accepts a
later prompt.

Maximum questions:

1. `Does any part of this still feel true?`
2. `What did you need, if you know?`
3. `What helped, even a little?`

Need prompts:

- boundaries
- connection
- autonomy
- rest or physical capacity
- something else
- not sure

The result becomes an editable Letter card:

```text
WHAT HAPPENED
user observation

WHAT I THINK I NEEDED
user-selected interpretation

WHAT HELPED
user-reported action and outcome

NEXT TIME
user-approved action or note
```

AI may suggest fields, but the user approves every interpretation.

## Personalization

The acute ranking order is:

1. a user-pinned action for this state
2. an action reported helpful in a similar context
3. a user-authored future-self note
4. a prepared boundary message
5. cautious generic content

Do not use a hidden score to state that an action works. Show observed history:

> You marked this helpful in 2 of 3 similar moments.

## Measurement

Measure product usefulness, not captured attention:

- time from Care entry to first meaningful feedback
- percentage reaching a real-world hand-off
- percentage choosing to leave after the hand-off
- later `better`, `same`, or `worse`
- whether Shatter leaves users calmer, unchanged, or more activated
- whether a returned personal action was available and used
- whether the user keeps, edits, or removes a future-self note
- urgent-safety and medical-routing discoverability

Do not optimize:

- total Care minutes
- cards viewed
- distress sessions per user
- notification re-entry into Care

## Evidence Boundary

Game-based emotion-regulation evidence is promising but heterogeneous and does
not validate these specific Letter scenes as treatment. Each scene requires
usability and adverse-response testing.

Physical Care follows two principles:

- familiar low-risk comfort actions may be returned as the user's own plan
- new, unusual, severe, changing, or function-limiting symptoms need a direct
  medical route rather than a more engaging game
